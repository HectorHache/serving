#The three errors I made, corrected here

  1. I claimed the guide contains no Modo Fácil. It does. It is on page 7, under the heading

MODO FÁCIL, with a full description. My earlier grep pattern used a wildcard that did not match the accented word. Both colleagues had it right and I was wrong. This is the most important correction in the document.

  1. I claimed the crafting system has no difficulty tiers and no fixed order. It has both. The

guide prints a 48 tier list in a fixed unlock order, and Gemini's independent per-item recipe counts match that list exactly, item for item.

  1. I logged the Zafra gym at the Prosperity Sanctuary and Rúpico TM034 as Light of Ruin. Zafra is

at Pueblo Bodegón on page 103, and TM034 is PANTALLA, Light Screen, on page 443.

#2. What the game actually is

Kalos, 300 years before Pokémon X and Y, during the Great War. King Aster Zephyr is building a machine that is supposed to return life to the dead. The war continues regardless. The king turns his invention into a weapon. Most of what happened is lost to history, and this game puts you in the middle of it.

You open inside a carriage bound for Canvas Town. A student the professor admitted has not turned up, and you have to go and find him through the fog. Before you leave the carriage you choose which generation to take your starter from. The guide recommends Kalos starters because they have new forms here, and states that every starter line was balanced to stay viable.

You then fight a wild regional Bidoof holding your companion hostage, pick one of three Poké Balls on the ground to choose which starter you use, and that choice is permanent.

The single most useful design fact about this game: it is not a Pokémon reskin. It replaces the type-specialist Gym with the Bastión, run by a Regente, who is a martial governor rather than a type specialist. Several use doubles. The badge is a medal. Collectibles unlock in a fixed order rather than being found in any order. Almost nothing is a hidden fetch quest except the things that are supposed to be.

#4. Modes, difficulty and quality of life

The guide is explicit on page 6: you can choose between three difficulty modes. Here they are in the order it lists them.

#The three difficulty modes

ModeWho it is forWhat it changes
Modo FácilNew to the franchiseRival and trainer Pokémon carry no EVs and no IVs
Modo NormalPlayers with series experienceThe balanced default
Modo HeroicoExperienced playersYou cannot see the next opposing Pokémon and cannot switch after a KO. Every rival Pokémon has perfect 31 IVs in all six stats. In-battle healing is banned, except effects from held items

Two details worth having. The guide states that in Normal and Heroico a rival starter arrives at level 5 regardless of difficulty, so the opening fight is fixed (p12). And it names the hard mode Heroico in the heading, then calls it Radical in six later passages, where the differences show up as concrete items: Reflect is too weak in Normal but fine in Radical (p30), Meditite gains an Eviolite in Radical (p36), and a specific Pokémon carries a Jaboca berry instead of an Oran berry in Radical (p38). Heroico is the official name. Radical is how the guide itself keeps referring to it.

#The three systems that sit outside the difficulty ladder

Random. Offered in the carriage, right before the starter question, on any new file after one full clear. Two variants, with or without randomised evolutions. Species, moves, abilities and held items are all randomised. The important bit: encounters are generated continuously on the fly, there is no fixed per-area random table, and difficulty scales progressively by route tier rather than randomising everything to the same chaos from the start (p5).

Monotype. You receive your starter in the carriage, then talk to the scientist with a star icon beside the Flare Laboratory in Canvas Town (p5). Three rules, all on p5: not every type is offered because some types have too few Pokémon, wild encounters do not change at all so the type does not thin out the world, and captures outside your type are simply blocked. A species is capturable if any stage of its evolutionary line touches your type.

Nuzlocke. Built in. A fainted Pokémon cannot be used again. Assisted Nuzlocke also exists: defeated Regents hand over Cenizas Sagradas, Sacred Ashes, which revive fallen partners (p5).

#The level cap is the spine of the whole difficulty design

A Pokémon at the cap for your current badge count gains exactly 1 EXP per battle. The in-game message is Has alcanzado el límite de nivel para el nº de medallas que tienes (p4). There is no grinding past a boss threshold, and the guide says so plainly.

#Quality of life

  • Infinite Rare Candies from a mug on the counter of every Pokémon Center (p4).
  • Shiny odds stated plainly at 0.001, one in 1000 (p4).
  • Day and night reads the host PC clock. The guide answers the question players actually ask,

which is why Umbreon will not evolve at night in your save (p4).

  • Controller rebinding on F1 (p4).
  • Your save survives updates, which matters a lot in a game with no version pinning.

The king builds a machine to restore life to the fallen. The war does not stop. Anger and desperation turn the machine into a weapon, and the Great War that X and Y later inherit is what is left.

#Antagonists

Team Azoth, the recurring enemy force. Rank-and-file members are Recluta Azoth. Named members include Félix, Alvar, Alexandra and Andrea. The alchemists carry Azoth as a codename with a surname: Núbila, Wolfram, Genos, Noelia, Elisa, Luciana, Margot, Liama, Benjamina, Viviana and Luna.

The royal gauntlet is four named members of the Luminalia court, Alto Caballero Tileo, Alta Dama Malva, Alta Dama Drácena and Alto Caballero Narciso. These are the X and Y Elite Four lightly rewritten to fit the era, which is the cheapest possible way to make a familiar structure feel native.

#The endgame spine

Three Yggdrasil legendaries carry the ending.

  1. Lysson, a Flare boss in the Deep Spring, then Xerneas at Manantial Profundo. Xerneas inflates

its own stats mid-battle unless you are holding its Effigy. The guide advises Ground types and a defensive approach.

  1. Yveltal behind the circus at Sanguine Town.
  2. Zygarde at the Eternal Tree.
  3. The twelve ancient druidic medals are brought together at northwest Luminalia, forming the Llave

Definitiva.

  1. Trainer Alca at level 100, then Alca plus the Ultimate Weapon at level 100 in doubles.
  2. Epilogue, 50 years later, the surviving cast meets for a commemorative photograph.

Auretosk is the fourth Yggdrasil legendary, Electric and Ghost, 126/131/95/99/98/131 for a 680 BST. Its ability, Aura Dorada, boosts every Electric move on the field by 30 percent, which completes a trio with Fairy Aura and Dark Aura. It raises its own stats during the battle. The guide's advice is one line: use Ground types, stay defensive.

#The endgame gauntlet is not where you expect

This is a detail two of the three passes got wrong. The four-member gauntlet equivalent to an Elite Four is Cámaras de Reflexión, not the Luminalia Palace. You find it by sitting in the middle of a six-seat table and using the Talismán de Malvo (p186). A talking Dusknoir explains the challenge. It keeps the same structure as the Luminalia Palace but the guide says it is more sinister and dark.

Three things about it are good design and worth stealing:

  • The four members can be fought in any order. Four teleporters sit in the four corners.
  • Each member has a stated theme, printed in the guide: Tileo takes the top left and focuses on

defensive Pokémon, Malva the top right and offensive ones, Drácena the bottom left and healing ones, Narciso the bottom right and status inflicting ones (p186-187).

  • The Alto Mando that follows is four more named fights: Tornasola, Aure, Prímula and Malvo, at

levels 98 to 99, then the King AZ dream trial at 99, then Alca at 100 (p412-421).

Two named fights award no medal at all, and both are optional, which is the detail most likely to cost someone a hundred hours:

  • Regente Arrayan, level 88, in the Luminalia crypts, guarded by Darkrai. If you beat Arrayan

before Xerneas, Xerneas does not give you the eleventh medal. The order matters.

  • Regente Siempreviva, level 88, in a dark web dungeon full of Galvantula and Yanmega and a Mega

Scolipede that sets Stealth Rock, also awarding nothing.

Constellar is the best idea in the Fakemon list. It needs a Solrock and a Lunatone in the party together and it fuses them, and then it has two forms that swap every time it uses Eclipse, which converts into physical or special depending on the form it is already in. That is a fusion requirement, a form mechanic and a signature move that only makes sense because of the form, in one species.

Royaleon breaks the stat budget on purpose. Realeza gives it plus 50 percent to every move of every type, which is the kind of ability that should be broken. It is the single strongest ability in the game and it is on an Eeveelution you get from a wig on an island.

#9. Mega Evolutions, 113 stones

#Gen 8 Gigantamax folded into the Mega framework

This is the single cleverest engineering decision in the game. Gen 8 had a mechanic, Dynamax, that would have collided with a Gen 6 engine. Instead of porting two systems, the developer made every Gigantamax form a bespoke Mega Stone: Butterfreeite, Machampite, Kinglerite, Laprasite, Garbodorita, Corviknightite, Orbeetleite, Drednawita, Coalossalita, Appletunite, Sandacondita, Toxtricitite, Centiskorchite, Hatterenita, Alcremita, Copperajahite, Rillaboomite, Cinderaceite and Inteleonite.

One mechanic, no visual fragmentation, and every Gigantamax keeps its stat boost. The guide confirms the magnitude: Mega Butterfree gets plus 150, Mega Garbodor plus 125, Mega Kingler plus 120.

#12. Crafting, 48 tiers and 50 items

The crafting system is the best single idea in this game and it is worth building your own version of. Forty-four Alchemy Pages are scattered across the region and found in any order, but the items they unlock come in a fixed order regardless of which page you found first.

That single rule changes what a collectible is. In most games a recipe book is a checklist you complete in whatever order you happen to explore. Here the pages are a permission slip, and the tier list is the progression track. You cannot rush the Mercury Key by finding page 40 first. The player knows exactly where they are in the track because the track is visible.

#The other systems in the same track

  • Nine Infused evolution stones, tiers 18 to 26, remove every trade evolution in the game. Alakazam,

Machamp, Golem and Gengar evolve in single player.

  • Seventeen type amulets spread from tier 0 to tier 40, one per type as cheap early progression.
  • Stat capsules, tiers 37 to 44, max individual IV and EV investment.
  • Shiny Potion at tier 46 guarantees the next wild or static encounter is shiny.
  • M Embryo at tier 47 is the endgame breeding catalyst for Mewtwo.
  • Master Ball at tier 45 puts a guaranteed catch behind almost the entire map.

If you build one system from this game, build the fixed-order unlock. The Mercury Key placement is the proof: tier 9 out of 47 is exactly late enough to feel earned and early enough to change the game.

The seventeen are a small number with a lot of surface. Each one is a single state machine with four states, and four states is an afternoon of work per resident:

  1. Unmet, standing at their map coordinate with dialogue and a prerequisite.
  2. Quest active, the player has triggered the flag.
  3. Recruited, the quest resolved and the NPC moved.
  4. Active in the hub, spawned at their stall with their service registered.

#14. How a Gen 6 engine was balanced to carry nine generations

This is the developer publishing his own work, which almost nobody does. A Google Sheet titled Lista de ajustes en Pokemon Z lists 636 species with the base stat, moves and ability changes against canonical values. Every row is in data/balance_adjustments.csv.

#The five rules the sheet applies

  1. Early game coverage. Bulbasaur, Ivysaur and Venusaur get Acid, Bounce and Fury Cutter, because

the classic line has no Poison STAB until level 46 and a Gen 6 era game will not wait that long.

  1. Trade gates removed. Every trade evolution evolves by level or with an Infused stone. Porygon at

level 30, Porygon-Z at level 50, no trading required.

  1. Mid-stage buffers. Middle evolutions get defensive buffs so they are not dead weight between

levels 40 and 45.

  1. Ability modernisation. Single-ability or dated species get modern ones. Archaludon gains Clear Body

as a hidden ability, Hydrapple gains Gooey.

  1. BST lifts into a usable band. Underpowered fully evolved species are raised into roughly the 500 to

540 range so they are viable at the top of the game.

The full sheet ranges from a 205 BST to a 734, so the ceiling goes above the canonical 680. Move pools are flattened too: Delibird plus 130, Feebas plus 115, Noibat plus 130, Tyrogue plus 110, and Delcatty gets plus 115 along with Fairy Skin.

The strategic lesson is not any single rule, it is the fact that the sheet exists at all. Publishing 636 balance changes with a stated rationale is simultaneously a design document, a community resource and a competitive advantage. Anyone building a creature battler with nine generations in the roster is solving this exact problem and this file is a free answer key.

#17. Mechanics worth stealing

This section is the reason to read the document if you are building something.

The 1 EXP clamp. Rather than hard-stopping experience at a cap, the engine intercepts the gain and returns 1 point. Nothing breaks, no artificial ceiling UI, no bug where a Pokémon at the cap refuses to gain anything and the game hangs. It is four lines of script and it removes an entire genre of exploit.

One invented mechanic that gates the map. The Mercury Key sits at tier 9 of a 47 tier crafting track and opens six or more major locations across the region. One number in one list decides the shape of the map behind it.

A collectible track with a visible position. Forty-four pages, fixed unlock order. The player can always see how far along the track they are.

A hub that grows. Seventeen services, each from one state machine with four states.

Collectibles that are the type chart. Eighteen plates, one per type, tied to eighteen bosses in eighteen different biomes, and the set as a whole gates the endgame legendary.

Doubles for two gyms out of twelve. Enough to break the pattern, cheap enough that it is not the game.

Trainers that scale to you. The circus on Route 22 puts ordinary trainers one level below your team average. Named bosses stay hardcoded at 85 to 88, so the scaling never trivialises the plot.

Trailers that cost no move slots. Crafted axe and powder replace Cut and Rock Smash. Nothing in the party exists only to open a door.

Trade gates removed wholesale. Nine Infused evolution stones plus level-based evolution for everything that used to need a second copy. Single player parity with the commercial games.

Difficulty that changes rules, not numbers. Heroico removes the next-Pokémon preview, removes switching, sets enemy IVs to 31 and bans bag healing. Modo Fácil sets enemy IVs and EVs to zero. Neither touches the level caps.

Megas and Gigantamax unified into one mechanic. Nineteen Gigantamax forms converted into Mega Stones rather than porting a second gimmick system into a Gen 6 engine.

Competitive tools handed out for free. Infinite Rare Candies, EV berries, nature mints, form changer, competitive held items, all reachable without grinding.

The battle presentation. Gen 5 animated battle sprites with dynamic scaling and custom entry animations, overworld followers that render your party leader, a dark themed UI with French royal heraldic flourishes, and custom audio arrangements of Kalos themes.

Two custom mechanics worth naming. Hemorrhage is a status that doubles the target critical rate, and the held item Periscopio combined with a high-crit move makes it a guaranteed application. Natures are printed on boss cards as explicit deltas, like -Atk +SpA, so you can read a boss intent before you commit.

A tide you control. One bell in the Prosperity Sanctuary raises and lowers the water on Routes 9 and

  1. One switch, two areas, three gated items and one legendary.

One completion reward that is itself a flex. Clear every achievement and get a Dark Lugia in Acrylic Town. Not a shiny one, a Dark one, which is the more interesting flex.

#19. What to copy, and what to watch

#Copy these nine things

  1. The fixed-order collectible track with a visible position.
  2. One invented mechanic that gates the whole map, placed at 20 percent progress.
  3. A hub that grows from seventeen single-purpose NPCs.
  4. The badge ladder tied to the TM ladder, so every reward is a verb.
  5. The 1 EXP clamp instead of a hard cap.
  6. A type-chart collectible set that gates the endgame legendary.
  7. Crafted traversal tools that cost no party slots.
  8. Publishing your balance spreadsheet with a stated rationale.
  9. A completion reward that is itself a flex.

#Watch these six things

  1. The English build lags the Spanish by five versions. Every balance and content fix since March

exists only in Spanish. Ship translations from a versioned source, or pin them visibly.

  1. The guide itself has a typo, at page 7, where it reads "Al activar el modo mantener" in the

Heroico paragraph. It should say the mode name. Ship a guide and someone will read the typo as a rule.

  1. Abilities get scrambled. Kadabra listed with Thick Fat reads as a bug, and longcat flagged it.

If you put abilities on boss cards, validate them against the ability table.

  1. Boss rosters published as images rather than text cost three research passes an entire day and

left 52 sprites unidentified. Print the species names in your guide.

  1. Non-respawning legendaries plus a one in 1000 shiny rate is friction on top of friction. The

warning is there and honest, which helps, but consider a postgame re-encounter table.

  1. Doubles and triples with hardcoded levels near 100 leave no room to underlevel. If your climax is

fixed at 98 to 100, the player who is three levels short has nothing to do but grind against the cap.
