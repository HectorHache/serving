Method and corrections

How every dataset was built, and every disagreement between the three research passes.

How each dataset was built, what it is good for, and what it is not. Read this before trusting a number.

#The primary source, and why it dominates

One document settles almost everything: the developer's own 559 page Spanish master guide, Guía Oficial de Pokémon Z, reached through the official documentation hub at pokemonzfangame.com/full-documentation/.

All 559 pages were text-extracted and are readable as plain text in ../sources/master-docs/master_guide_full.txt. Page markers are preserved in the form [PAGE n], so any claim in the final document can be traced back to the exact page.

Everything else is secondary. The official site's 21 posts confirm and extend the guide. The developer's balance spreadsheet confirms the numbers. Where the guide and a colleague disagree, the guide wins, and the disagreement is logged rather than silently resolved.

#The sprite identification problem

The single biggest limitation in this research, and the reason 52 rows are marked UNKNOWN.

The guide prints boss trainer cards as a table. Every cell is text except the Pokémon sprite, which is an image. So for 427 roster rows you get the level, held item, ability, nature and all four moves in perfect text, and the species itself only as a picture.

Method used: extract all 426 sprites, match them by eye against 988 official artwork files, then sanity check each identification against the ability, the moveset and the held item that are printed beside it.

375 of 427 identified. 52 remain UNKNOWN. For those the sprite path is kept in the dataset next to the row, so you can open the image and try.

Two rows carry a competing identification in the alt_identification column, marked "guide art + Gemini second opinion (unverified)". Treat those as a coin flip, not a fact.

#What is single sourced

Three things all three passes agree on, and all three are wrong to agree on, because they read one file:

  1. Mega stats for the three starter Megas. The guide does not print them. All three passes carry the

same numbers because they all read the same JSON file. That is one source wearing three hats.

  1. The Pokémon Essentials version. One colleague wrote v21.1, the other v19-v20. The official

download page says only "built with RPG Maker XP and Pokemon Essentials". No version is published anywhere. Unresolvable.

  1. The 100 hour playtime figure. A developer estimate, not a measurement.

#What was not attempted

  • The game files themselves were never downloaded, extracted or decompiled. Everything here comes from

the published guide, the official site, the published spreadsheet, and artwork matching.

  • No attempt was made to verify anything against a running copy of the game.

#Second pass

A parser can only report what it understood, so tmp/build/verify.py re-reads the parsed output against the raw guide and fails loudly on anything that does not hold up. It is wired into the build, so a regression stops the site being rebuilt rather than shipping quietly. All ten checks pass:

PASS  All 419 step page numbers exist in the source
PASS  Every linked species exists in the Pokedex
PASS  Every encounter species is printed on its cited page
PASS  All 76 distinct drop names occur in the guide text
PASS  Medal gates 1-10 all detected
PASS  Every medal page really contains a MEDALLA heading
PASS  Every step with items has an "Objetos obtenibles" header on that page
PASS  Trainer level bands are within 1-100 and ordered
PASS  Catch index species all exist in the Pokedex
PASS  Available species count never goes backwards

The CSS gets the same treatment. Tailwind v4 silently drops a whole declaration when it meets var(--color-ink-800/50), which is invalid outside a Tailwind-emitted utility, so audit_css() fails the build on that pattern. A post-build audit in the browser confirms zero broken artwork, and every page was opened and exercised before this was called done.

#The type chart

No source for this game prints its own type chart. Neither the developer guide, nor Gemini's dossier, nor the longcat research, nor the official site. What all three research passes agree on is that the game keeps ordinary STAB mechanics and swaps Dynamax for Mega Stones, so the balance work is in stats and moves rather than in type interactions. The matrix therefore uses the canonical 18-type chart, and says so on the page rather than implying it was read out of the game.

tmp/build/typechart.py self-checks all 324 cells, and the page build asserts that every type present in the Pokédex exists in the chart. That check earned its keep: it caught a missing Fighting column in the Ground row before the page ever shipped.

#Corrections found by testing rather than reading

Three faults survived a clean build and only showed up when the pages were exercised in a browser:

  1. Battle advice was being dropped on every multi-page gym. The parser only

kept a Consejos de batalla: block when a battle record was still open, but a gym roster usually runs over two or three pages, so the tactics landed on a page with no open battle and were discarded. Fixed by keeping the advice on the step as well. Steps carrying advice went from 14 to 68, and 10 of the 11 medal gates now carry their fight's tactics.

  1. The compact matrix never highlighted anything. It stored the chosen type's

name and compared it against cell indices, so 6 === 'fighting' was false for all 324 cells. It lit up 0 cells and dimmed all 324.

  1. Two functions called label. The type-name helper shadowed the species-name

helper, so rendering a card threw. Both renamed.

A fourth was caught by a build-time assertion rather than by looking: the type chart's Ground row was missing its Fighting column.

#Reproducing this

The extraction and merge scripts are in ../../tmp/build/. build_data.py regenerates every dataset in data/ from the guide text and the colleague directories. It reads, it never writes to any source.

To check any page reference in the final document:

grep -n "PAGE 447" ../../sources/master-docs/master_guide_full.txt

#Deliberate omissions

The guide is 559 pages of walkthrough. This document keeps the systems, the numbers and the design lessons, and drops the step-by-step route text. If you are walking the game, use the guide. If you are studying it or building something like it, the route text is noise.

Three passes researched the same game from the same public material and disagreed in roughly thirty places. This file is the audit trail. If you want to check my work, start here, because every row is a claim with a page number attached.

#3. The error I made, and both colleagues had right

I claimed the guide contains no Modo Fácil. It does. Page 7, under the heading MODO FÁCIL:

MODO FÁCIL: si eres nuevo a la franquicia, este modo de juego es perfecto para ti. Los Pokémon de los entrenadores rivales no tienen puntos de esfuerzo (EVs) ni puntos de estadística (IVs).

The guide lists exactly three difficulty modes on page 6: puedes elegir entre tres modos de dificultad. Fácil, Normal, Heroico.

My earlier search used a wildcard pattern that did not match the accented word. Two colleagues reported the mode from other sources and I dismissed both. That was the wrong call.

A second related detail I had backwards: I wrote that the guide calls the hard mode Radical. It calls it Heroico in the heading and then refers to it as Radical in six later passages, where the differences are concrete: Reflect is too weak in Normal but fine in Radical (page 30), Meditite gains an Eviolite in Radical (page 36), a specific Pokémon carries a Jaboca berry instead of an Oran in Radical (page 38). Heroico is the heading. Radical is the nickname.

#5. Level caps, confirmed by cross-check

Gemini published a 13-row cap ladder: 15, 25, 35, 44, 53, 62, 70, 78, 85, 90, 94, 97, 100.

I had derived the first ten independently from each Bastión ace level. Every one matches. Canola aces at 15 and the next cap is 25. Hisopo at 25, next 35. F3 at 35, next 44. And so on to Cendera at 90. The last three continue the same curve into the legendary arcs and the league.

Gemini's labelling is offset by one, since they read "Badge 0" for the starting cap. The ladder itself is right.

#7. Alpha Nests, all 18 verified, and the guide has an appendix for them

Neither colleague knew the guide lists all 18 in one place. Pages 447 and 448 do, and every location and access condition from longcat's table matches. The surf requirements on the Electric and Water nests and the mount requirement on the Grass nest are all confirmed.

The plate names are Gemini's contribution and none of them contradict the guide.

One Gemini conflict: their nest list gives Fist Plate for both Normal and Fighting. There are two distinct plates, Tabla Fuerte and Tabla Puño.

#9. Claims nobody could check

Two items appear in a colleague document with no page reference and no obvious source. Neither is repeated as fact here.

  • A route 26 Shiny Lugia for clearing all achievements. The guide says the reward is a

Lugia Oscuro, a Dark Lugia. Corrected.

  • A claim that Route 26 grass holds a specific seven species list. Plausible, consistent with a

#The lesson worth keeping

Three passes made roughly thirty errors between them and every single one had the same cause: a claim was written down, then the next claim was written beside it, and somewhere in the middle the two merged into something nobody had checked. The Zafra gym is the clearest example. It sat in the middle of a table, drifted from Pueblo Bodegón to the Prosperity Sanctuary, and then got copied forward into a downstream merge by someone who never opened the page.

Page numbers are not bureaucracy. They are the only thing that distinguishes a fact from a sentence that sounds like one.
